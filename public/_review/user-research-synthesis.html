Section 2 · Four Forces Diagram

Push, Pull, Anxiety, Habit

Illustrative

F1 · Push

  • Standups fragment maker time
  • Remote members repeat updates across timezones
  • Status meetings hide real blockers
  • Meeting load makes managers feel busy but not informed

F2 · Pull

  • FunctionalFewer synchronous interruptions
  • FunctionalSearchable status history
  • EmotionalLess performative meeting anxiety
  • SocialLooks like a disciplined distributed team

F3 · Anxiety of the New

  • Will people actually write useful updates?
  • Can managers trust async accountability?
  • Will important blockers be missed?
  • Will this become yet another tool?

F4 · Habit of the Present

  • Daily standup is a ritual everyone recognizes
  • Leaders equate live presence with control
  • Slack pings are already where work happens
  • Teams know how to cancel a meeting, not redesign coordination

Section 5 · Job Clusters

Hiring pathways (by pushes + pulls)

Illustrative

Job 1 — When a distributed product team keeps repeating status across meetings and Slack, help them preserve alignment without sacrificing maker mornings.

Push cluster: meeting repetition, timezone drag, status theater.

Pull cluster: single async artifact, searchable blockers, less interruption.

Conflict: serving this job with strict prompts may hurt teams that need emotional check-in rituals.

Job 2 — When managers cannot see blockers until it is too late, help them create accountability without adding surveillance.

Push cluster: late surprises, vague ownership, stakeholder pressure.

Pull cluster: blocker visibility, follow-up trails, calmer escalation.

Conflict: too much manager visibility may hurt IC trust and adoption.

Job 3 — When senior ICs lose deep-work continuity to ritual meetings, help them communicate progress without performing availability.

Push cluster: fragmented focus, performative updates, calendar resentment.

Pull cluster: concise check-ins, autonomy, fewer context switches.

Conflict: minimizing participation may hurt managers who need richer context.

Job 4 — When a growing remote team onboards new members, help them learn norms without reviving daily live status meetings.

Push cluster: new-hire uncertainty, undocumented process, ritual drift.

Pull cluster: templates, examples, persistent team memory.

Conflict: template guidance may feel bureaucratic to mature teams.
