The whitespace hypothesis sits between story and experience: outcomes that begin before the wedding and remain useful afterward.

Three $3K+ service hypotheses

$3.5K–$6K01 · Legacy

Family Archive Edition

Pre-wedding family interviews, digitized heirloom images, wedding-day portraits, and a designed archive shared across generations.

Why it may win: the deliverable is a family-history product, not a larger image count.

$3K–$5K02 · Speed

48-Hour Story Room

A same-weekend editorial team produces a private web story, short vertical sequence, and share-ready guest gallery while attention is highest.

Why it may win: compresses time-to-memory and gives couples a controlled alternative to scattered guest posts.

$4K–$8K03 · Continuity

First-Year Documentary

Wedding coverage plus three scheduled chapters—homecoming, first celebration, and anniversary—delivered as one coherent visual narrative.

Why it may win: extends a single high-trust booking into a year-long relationship.

Customer tensions

  • Couples want distinctive memories but struggle to evaluate package differences.
  • Fast social sharing can conflict with deliberate storytelling.
  • Families hold context and archives that traditional coverage misses.

Consulting caution: these are research hypotheses. Interviews and paid pilots must verify willingness to pay.

30-day validation

  1. Interview 8 recently married couples across budget tiers.
  2. Show three one-page concept cards; ask for ranking and trade-offs.
  3. Pre-sell one narrowly scoped pilot for each top concept.
  4. Track proposal-to-deposit, delivery effort, referral intent, and gross margin.
